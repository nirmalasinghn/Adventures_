Fitness for Western Ghats Hikes

You don't need to be an athlete to hike the Western Ghats. You need to prepare your body for the trail.

Understanding Fitness

Fitness for hiking isn't about how you look. It's about what your body can comfortably do.

  • Western Ghats hikes may not need Himalayan-style altitude preparation, but they still ask a lot of your body.
  • Steep climbs, long descents, mud, heat and humidity add up.
  • You don't need to be strong or fast. You need to move comfortably for hours and recover well.
  • The goal is simple: be comfortable, capable and prepared on the trail.
Kudremukha. Hours of steady climbing — this is what the training is for.
What to Train

Six things that actually help

01

Cardio: Keep Moving

The goal isn't to run a marathon. It's to keep moving without feeling completely drained.

  • Brisk walking
  • Cycling
  • Swimming
  • Stairs
  • Sports
  • Running (optional)
02

Strong Legs: Climb + Descend

Getting to the top is only half the job. Your legs also need to handle the descent.

  • Squats
  • Lunges
  • Wall sit
  • Glute bridges
03

Balance: Find Your Footing

Hiking isn't just strength. It's controlling your body on roots, loose rock and wet ground.

  • Tree pose (Vrikshasana)
  • Ankle strength
  • Walking on uneven ground
04

Mobility: Move Naturally

Mobility supports hiking, but it doesn't replace strength and endurance.

  • Yoga
  • Downward dog
  • Dynamic warm-ups
  • Ankles, hips, hamstrings
05

Carry What You Need

Don't wait until hike day to find out what carrying a backpack for hours feels like.

  • Start with a light pack
  • Build up gradually
  • Keep it light
06

Train for the Trail

Your body needs to learn how to move, not just exercise.

  • Hills
  • Stairs
  • Parks
  • Trails
  • Heat & humidity

Part Two

I'm a Complete Beginner.
How Do I Prepare?

You don't need to get fit before you start. Start where you are, then build gradually.

  • 30–40 minute walks, 4–5 active days a week
  • 2 basic strength sessions a week
  • Squats, lunges, wall sit, glute bridges, bird dog, plank

Keep repetitions manageable. Controlled movement and showing up matter most.

  • 40–60 minute brisk walks, with inclines and stairs
  • A longer walk at the weekend

Optional, if you enjoy running:

3 min brisk walk → 1 min easy jog → repeat

Running is optional. You don't need to become a runner to become a good hiker.

  • Longer walks on hills, stairs and uneven ground
  • A light backpack
  • Build up to several hours outdoors

Increase difficulty a little at a time. Stay in any stage as long as you need.

Exercise Library

Eight movements. Start → Build → Progress.

Tap a card for how to do it. No magic rep numbers: start where it feels controlled.

Squat Quads + glutes How to

Sit your hips back and down like onto a chair. Chest tall, knees over toes, press through your whole foot.

Start: squat to a chair.
Build: bodyweight squats.
Progress: lower slowly or add a light pack.
Reverse Lunge Leg strength + balance How to

Step one foot back and lower the back knee toward the floor. Keep your torso upright, then push through the front foot.

Start: hold a wall, small steps.
Build: alternate legs, no support.
Progress: slower, or hold a light weight.
Wall Sit Quads + leg endurance How to

Lean your back against a wall and slide down until your knees bend, as far as feels comfortable. Keep your knees over your ankles and breathe.

Start: a shallow slide, hold briefly.
Build: slide lower, hold a little longer.
Progress: longer holds with rests between.
Bird Dog Core + balance + glutes How to

On hands and knees, reach one arm forward and the opposite leg back. Keep your hips level and your back flat. Switch sides.

Start: lift only the leg, or only the arm.
Build: lift the opposite arm and leg together.
Progress: pause for a few breaths at full reach.
Downward Dog Hamstrings + calves + shoulders How to

From hands and knees, lift your hips up and back so your body makes an upside-down V. Press your hands into the floor, bend your knees as much as you need, and let your heels sink toward the floor. Breathe slowly.

Start: knees well bent, heels off the floor.
Build: straighten your legs a little at a time.
Progress: hold for a few slow breaths.
Glute Bridge Glutes + hips How to

Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Press through your heels and lift your hips until shoulders, hips and knees line up. Lower slowly.

Start: a small lift.
Build: pause at the top.
Progress: one leg at a time.
Tree Pose Vrikshasana Balance + stability How to

Shift your weight onto one foot. Place the other foot on your calf or inner thigh, never on the knee. Stand tall, fix your eyes on one spot and breathe. Switch sides.

Start: toes resting on the floor by your ankle, a hand on the wall.
Build: foot on the calf, palms together at your chest.
Progress: foot on the inner thigh, arms overhead.
Plank Core stability How to

Forearms down, elbows under shoulders, body in one long line. Breathe steadily and stop when your form slips.

Start: hands on a bench, or knees down.
Build: short forearm holds with rests.
Progress: slightly longer holds, or side planks.

Highlighted = the area being worked. Sharp pain means stop.

Mental Fitness

Your mind is part of your hiking gear

  • Stay calm when tired
  • Know when to slow down
  • Don't compare your pace
  • Speak up to your group
  • Respect weather and trail conditions
  • Don't let group pressure override safety
Reaching the summit is optional. Returning safely is not.
Listen to Your Body

Normal exertion vs warning signs

Normal exercise responses

  • Breathing harder and sweating
  • Tired muscles, mild soreness
  • Needing short breaks

Stop & take serious symptoms seriously

  • Chest pain or fainting
  • Severe dizziness or confusion
  • Severe or unusual shortness of breath
  • Loss of coordination or severe weakness

If you have a medical condition, significant symptoms, or concerns about starting a new exercise program, seek appropriate medical advice before beginning.

Two Kinds of Mountains

Western Ghats vs Himalayan fitness

Western Ghats

Typical 1-day hike

  • Steep gradients, repeated climbs and descents
  • Uneven, slippery, muddy terrain
  • Heat, humidity and monsoon conditions
Himalayan treks

Can additionally involve

  • Much greater altitude and acclimatisation
  • Lower temperatures
  • Multi-day load carrying and different recovery demands

Different terrain. Different preparation.

Ready to Start?

You don't need to wait until you're "fit enough" to begin. Start with regular walks, build your strength, explore stairs and hills, and gradually spend more time outdoors.

Leave No Trace

The Trail Gives.
Don't Take More Than You Should.

Pack out everything you carry in; the mountain is not a bin. Carry a steel water bottle. Stay on the marked trail; one shortcut by a hundred people becomes a decade of erosion. Keep the noise down. The wildlife here is wild. Let it stay that way.

Train for the trail. Return safely.
